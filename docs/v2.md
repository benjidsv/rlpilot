# v2: the clean base

v2 restarts the Python side from scratch, on the `v2` branch (v1 stays on `main`). The goal is unchanged:
**drive tracks never seen in training**, safe before fast. Results aren't compared with v1.

Phase 1 is the base: workers, starts, evaluation, a CleanRL trainer, and a deliberately plain observation and
reward. Phase 2 designs the observation, the reward and the steering, judged on the evaluation. The observation is done.

## Tracks

There are 41 tracks with 231 km of road (`data/split-v2.json`, seed 20261122):

| Split | Tracks | Road |
|---|---|---|
| **Test** | Nordschleife, Pinabashi, Tuna | 26.4 km |
| **Validation** | Alicante, Dijon, Espie, Karwada, Street-1, Torino | 28.0 km |
| **Train** | the other 32, including Spring and Longjour | 172 km |

- **Test:** the Nordschleife, plus one short circuit and one short road track, so that one track's character doesn't
  decide the result. It's touched once, at the very end (`eval.py --test`). The Nordschleife was a v1 test track: v1
  weights failed there, but v2's have never seen it.
- **Validation:** 3 medium circuits and 3 medium road tracks. It selects checkpoints and guides the work.
- **Train:** everything else.

Training spreads its samples **per metre of road, not per track**. Workers are allocated to tracks in proportion to
length (at least one each), so Spring gets about 7 of 48 and Pinabashi-sized tracks get 1. Each worker keeps its track.
On the Mac, rollouts run at 24k steps/s with 20 workers and 33k with 48, so more workers cost nothing.

## Actions and the gearbox

An action is `[steer, pedal, shift]`.

- **Steer:** a change of the steering command, at most 0.0625 per step (placeholder).
- **Pedal:** > 0 throttle, < 0 brake.
- **Shift:** a discrete choice of down, hold or up. The policy is hybrid: a Gaussian over steer and pedal, and a
  categorical over the shift, initialized to hold about 91% of the time.

The gearbox is sequential: R, 1, 2, ..., 6. Its rules:
- After a shift, the next one is allowed 5 steps (0.2 s) later. The car takes 0.1 s to engage a gear, then 0.1 s to
  release the clutch.
- A downshift that would take the engine past the redline is refused.
- Moving between 1st and reverse is only allowed below 1 m/s.

All of this logic lives in `controls.py`. The worker just applies the gear it's sent, and slips the clutch when pulling
away. It also reports the rpm the gears above and below would give.

Note: v1's worker misread the simulator's gear count and never shifted past 5th. Fixed in v2.

## Observation

The rule: **what a driver gets**. That includes the track map, like a driver who knows the track or reads pace notes.
It's 105 inputs (`obs.py`, where `NAMES` lists them with their scales).

- **The car (21):** speed (forward, sideways), yaw rate, accelerations x/y/z in the car's frame with gravity in them (what
  the driver feels), heading to the road and offset from its middle, rpm and the rpm one gear up and down, the gear,
  the shift cooldown, and the steer and pedal applied at the previous step.
- **The road's shape (84):** both edges, in the car's frame (x forward, y left), at 21 points along the centreline:
  20, 10, 5 and 2 m behind (the mirrors, for backing out), then 0, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 40, 55, 70, 90, 115, 145, 180,
  220, 260 and 300 m ahead. The bot's braking zones reach 311 m on Hidden Valley and 260 m on the Nordschleife.

Not in it, on purpose: rays, the bot's line or speed, the distance along the lap, the track, a timer (the time limit is
bootstrapped), wheel loads and slip.

**How it was chosen** (3M tests on a 10M learning-rate schedule, one seed each, 2026-11-22/23):

| Run | Inputs | Validation nominal survival | Crashed completion |
|---|---|---|---|
| verify (phase 1: rays + 10 edge points) | 79 | 0.61 (at 5M) | 0.38 |
| full: edges + height, runoff, surface at every point | 273 | 0.20 (at 2.5M) | 0.45 (at 2.5M) |
| full with relu / first-layer gain 0.5 | 273 | 0.21 / 0.23 | 0.48 / 0.59 |
| lean: extras at 9 points | 177 | 0.26 | 0.51 |
| **geometry: edges only (this)** | **105** | **0.58** | **0.64** |
| compact: edges + 24 extras at fixed scales | 129 | 0.46 | 0.71 |

- **Rays aren't needed.** They only measured the road's edge, and read 0 as soon as the car was off the road. Without
  them the car learns about as well, and completes far more crashed starts.
- **Extras at every point hurt:** they drown the edges in the first layer (the full observation's variance sits in 9
  directions out of 273, and it starts 16% of CleanRL's tanh units saturated).
- **Compact extras trained better but evaluated worse** than geometry: fewer clean scenarios, more lost control, a slower
  pace (paired over the suite, p 0.001-0.012 on nominal, mild and disturbed). A guess, unchecked: knowing the runoff, it
  learns that running wide is cheap, which training barely punishes and the evaluation counts as losing control.

The extras are left for later ablations. The full observation is in git (ebc2fd2); the lean and compact variants are
in `runs/obs-variants-2026-11-23.py` (not in git). The track table still has everything they need: each edge's
height, the runoff's width, and its surface (`grass`, `loose` for sand, gravel and dirt, or paved, from the material
names in `tracks.surface_kind`). `obs.FIXED` marks
inputs at a fixed scale that the running normalization must skip (none for now); it's saved in checkpoints as
`obs_fixed`. `train.py` has `--activation` (tanh, relu) and `--first-gain` from the tests, and `--anneal-timesteps` to
run a long run's schedule in a short test.

## Reward

Per step (`reward.py`, each component logged as `4_driving/reward_<name>`). **Closed 2026-11-23: the phase-1 reward
stays** (the defaults); the knobs below stay in `train.py` for later, judged on the model's behaviour.

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| progress | 0.1 per metre along the track, signed (driving backwards costs). Nothing while all four wheels are off the road. `--best-only-progress`: only past the furthest point reached this episode. `--progress-units bot_seconds`: each metre weighted by 40 m/s / the bot's speed there. |
| damage | -0.001 per point of damage |
| control | 0 by default. `--lost-control-cost`: charged each time the car loses control, by any of: a new contact, starting to face backwards, going more than 3 m beyond the edge (`--lost-control-causes`) |
| terminal | -10 when the episode fails (`--terminal`) |

- **Losing control** uses the evaluation's own tests, so a "recovered" scenario is exactly one the reward charged (or
  that started out of control). The cost is charged once, when the car enters the state, not for as long as it lasts:
  a long recovery mustn't cost more than giving up. A state the launch began in (a crashed start facing backwards)
  isn't charged. -2 is worth 20 m of progress, about half a second.
- **Progress pays only new ground** (`--best-only-progress`, off by default since the sweep). Reversing costs nothing, and driving back
  over metres already covered pays nothing. Before, progress was signed, and reversing off the road cost reward that
  couldn't be earned back there, so backing out of the grass was a net loss.
- **Why** (2026-11-23): geometry-3m's validation nominal starts were 54% clean, 4% survived, **36% recovered** and only
  6% failed. That is, the headline was lost to losing control, not to failing. The phase-1 reward barely saw it: in a
  30 s training episode, progress earned +86, failure cost -2 on average and damage cost -0.4. Of the 50 recovered
  scenarios, 37 lost control first by contact, 13 by going far off, none by facing backwards. Contacts are mostly
  brushes: a median of 14 damage points (75% under 57). Espie and Torino have the most.
- **First test, control-3m** (cost 2 + best-only, against geometry-3m): train nominal survival 0.44 -> 0.54 (p < 0.001)
  and half the time off the road, but validation nominal flat (0.58 -> 0.56), its completion 0.94 -> 0.84, and crashed
  completion 0.64 -> 0.41. The car **gives up**: crashed starts facing backwards sit still until the no-progress rule
  ends them, and cars that went far off stop there. The argument for a one-off cost was wrong: the -10 for no progress
  comes 10 s later, worth 0.995^250 = 0.29 of it, about -2.9 now, barely more than one event.
- **The reward sweep** (`scripts/sweep.py`, 2026-11-23 night): 14 variants x 3 seeds of 3M, against the phase-1 reward
  (`base`): the cost alone and best-only alone, the cost's size (0.5, 1, 2), a dearer failure (terminal 20, gamma
  0.998), facing backwards uncharged, progress in bot-seconds. `scripts/sweep_report.py` reads it. Training-only knobs:
  `--terminal`, `--lost-control-causes`, `--progress-units`, `--gamma`; the eval's rules never change.
- **Sweep results** (validation, mean of 3 seeds; z = difference from base / the seed noise, |z| >= 2 is real):

  | Variant | Nominal survival (seeds) | z | Nominal completion | Crashed completion | Pace |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | botsec-cost1 | 0.648 (0.636-0.664) | +1.9 | 0.945 | 0.567 | 0.920 |
  | cost2-g998 | 0.617 (0.493-0.693) | +1.2 | 0.929 | 0.521 | 0.922 |
  | botsec | 0.605 (0.543-0.671) | +0.9 | 0.938 | 0.657 | 0.886 |
  | cost1 | 0.593 (0.564-0.636) | +0.6 | 0.905 | 0.552 | 0.981 |
  | t20 | 0.588 (0.564-0.600) | +0.5 | 0.905 | 0.550 | 0.972 |
  | **base** | 0.564 (0.521-0.629) | | 0.931 | 0.550 | 0.976 |
  | best | 0.548 (0.486-0.593) | -0.4 | 0.917 | 0.617 | 0.970 |

  (cost0.5, cost1-t20, cost2-t20, g998 and cost2-nobackwards are between 0.545 and 0.579: `sweep_report.py` has all.)
  - **Giving up comes from the cost**, not from best-only progress: cost 2 drops nominal completion 0.93 -> 0.86
    (z -2.2), with or without facing backwards charged. Terminal 20 fixes half of it, gamma 0.998 all of it.
  - **Bot-seconds and gamma 0.998 drive slower** (pace -0.09, z -4; -0.05, z -2.5). Over the metre runs, slower is
    safer (+0.007 survival per 1% of pace): at botsec-cost1's pace that predicts 0.605, so ~+0.04 of its +0.083 isn't
    just slowness.
  - **Crashed starts:** bot-seconds (+0.10) and best-only (+0.07) raise completion on both splits (z ~1 each).
- **10M test** (botsec-best-cost1 vs base, 3 seeds each, an eval every 2M; `sweep_report.py --steps 10000000 --curve`):

  | Validation, 10M | Nominal survival (sd) | Crashed completion | Pace |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | base | 0.626 (0.025) | 0.636 | 1.002 |
  | botsec-best-cost1 | 0.657 (0.057) | 0.564 | 0.915 |
  | difference (z) | +0.031 (+0.9) | -0.071 (-1.3) | -0.087 (-2.8) |

  Train agrees (+0.037, z +1.0; pace -0.10, z -3.6). Its nominal survival lead was +0.12 at 2M, +0.08 at 4M, then
  +0.01 to +0.04 from 6M: bot-seconds learns a careful driver fast and then stays flat (0.64-0.66), while base is still
  climbing at 10M (0.52 -> 0.63, crashed completion 0.52 -> 0.64). The pace gap doesn't close (0.89 -> 0.915). Per seed,
  survival follows slowness (botsec seed 3: pace 0.85, 0.700; seed 2: pace 0.94, 0.593, below every base seed), and
  the slower-is-safer trend predicts +0.06 for its pace, more than its +0.03. **It buys survival with pace**, so the
  round closes on the phase-1 reward. Cost 1 and terminal 20 (+0.03 each at 3M, z ~0.5) aren't worth a default.
- **Seed noise** (the first measurement, from this sweep): validation nominal survival moves by an sd of **0.055** at
  3M (0.044 at 10M) from seed to seed (base: 0.521, 0.543, 0.629), train 0.052, crashed completion 0.11, pace 0.027. Two 3-seed means need
  ~0.09 apart to be 2 standard errors. Compare.py's sign test can't judge a setup: two seeds of one setup reach p < 0.05
  in 11 of 42 pairs on validation (18 on train). **Every comparison now runs 3 seeds** (`sweep.py`'s default). Training
  is deterministic (the same seed and code give the same CSV), but code changes move it: base seed 1 doesn't reproduce
  geometry-3m.

## Episodes and starts

Training episodes last 30 s of sim time, then the time limit cuts them. The agent bootstraps through the cut: it is not
treated as a crash.

An episode **fails** (and gets -10) when:
- there is **no new best distance along the track for 10 s**,
- or damage passes 5000,
- or the engine ends the race.

There are no stuck, backwards or off-track clocks. Spinning, reversing out of the grass or a three-point turn are fine,
as long as the car gets going again.

Kinds of start (`starts.py`), with their training shares:

| Kind | Share | What |
|---|---|---|
| nominal | 50% | on the bot's line, at the bot's speed there (±10%), facing down the road |
| perturbed | 25% | across 80% of the road, 0 to 1.2× the bot's speed, heading error up to 30° when slow and a few degrees when fast |
| disturbed | 12% | a nominal start, then 0.3-0.8 s of forced bad input (a steering jerk, a brake stab, or a throttle stab with steering), run inside the reset |
| crashed | 8% | nearly stopped, facing any way, across the road or up to 3 m beyond its edge (inside the barriers) |
| grid | 5% | a standing start |

The bounds are physical, so nominal starts are feasible by construction: the bot drove that spot at that speed. The
diagnostic `episodes/failed_near_start_<kind>` shows when a kind of start is too hard (failed without ever
getting 50 m down the road). Every launch starts
with the wheels straight.

## Evaluation

The suite (`data/suite-v2.csv`, 2855 scenarios) has start points every 200 m on validation and test tracks, and every
600 m on training tracks. Each start point has one scenario per tier: nominal, mild (eval only), perturbed, disturbed and
crashed. A scenario drives **500 m along the track**, within 60 s.

Every launch was checked once in the engine, and the few that came back non-finite were redrawn. Every checkpoint of
every run plays exactly this list, so **results are reproducible**: the same checkpoint gives the same CSV. Each
scenario is a fresh race, so the worker layout doesn't matter (48 and 64 workers give byte-identical CSVs).

**Speed** (validation + train, 2195 scenarios, Mac, 48 workers): down from 65-75 s to 48 s standalone for
geometry-3m's checkpoint, and about 36 s in training, where the workers are already up. A worse policy takes longer, since failed scenarios run their full 10 s without progress. Three changes,
with identical results:
- A scenario's reset is sent without waiting for it: the race restart (~8 ms) used to hold up every worker, 2195 times.
  The kick steps of disturbed starts run in the other envs' exchanges (`RacingEnv(overlap_starts=True)`, native
  `rlp_send` / `rlp_wait` / `rlp_answered`).
- Workers are spread to minimize the busiest one's scenarios (`evaluate.spread`). Before, every track got a worker, and
  Spring's 2 workers played 92 scenarios each against an average of 46. That's 30k batch steps instead of 37k.
- `train.py` keeps the eval's workers up for the whole run (`evaluate.Suite`), so it doesn't spend 12 s starting them
  for every eval. That's 48 more processes at ~42 MB each. `eval.py` still starts its own (`run_suite`).
- 64 workers aren't faster on 8 cores: fewer batch steps (20k), but each costs more, and the workers take 4 s longer to
  start. The simulation is only 0.07 ms per step per worker. A batch step's cost is mostly Python, the policy and the
  sync.

Outcome of a scenario, best first:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| clean | reached 500 m, never fully off the road, never lost control |
| survived | reached 500 m, went fully off the road but kept control |
| recovered | reached 500 m, but lost control on the way: hit something (took damage), faced backwards, or went more than 3 m beyond the edge |
| failed | didn't reach 500 m |

The CSV also records how a scenario lost control: `lost_first` (the first cause), the number of times it entered each
state (`contact`, `backwards`, `far_off`) and its `damage`. `eval.py` prints the recovered scenarios by first cause.

Metrics, per split (`3_eval/<split>/...` in W&B):
- **`nominal/survival`** (clean or survived) is the headline, and it selects `best.pt` on validation. It comes with a 90%
  interval from a bootstrap over tracks.
- **`<tier>/survival`, `completion`, `clean`** for each tier. For crashed starts, read `completion`: they begin out of
  control.
- **`nominal/lost_<cause>`:** the share of nominal scenarios that entered that state.
- **`pace`:** the median of bot time / agent time over the survived nominal scenarios. Above 1 means faster than the bot.
- **Grid laps** (`eval.py --grid-laps`): 2 laps from a standing start, the real task. It's reported, but not used to
  select checkpoints.

**Weights & Biases** (project RLPilot) replaced TensorBoard. `train.py` logs there as it trains (`--track`, on by default;
`--no-track` logs nothing, the eval CSVs are still written). The run is named after its folder and grouped by
`--exp-name`, so the seeds of one setup show as one line (their mean) with a band. Every metric is plotted against
`global_step`. Once per machine: `uv run wandb login`. Offline (`WANDB_MODE=offline`), then `wandb sync
runs/<run>/wandb/offline-run-*` uploads it. The metrics, in numbered sections:

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| `2_ppo` | KL, clip fraction, explained variance, entropy, action std, losses, learning rate |
| `3_eval` | every tier of both splits |
| `4_driving` | training episodes: speed, ends, shifts, reward components, losses of control by cause |
| `5_perf` | steps/s, eval time |

Every v2 run from before the switch is uploaded too (from its TensorBoard events, which stay in its folder).

The charts are laid out in code, `scripts/wandb_workspace.py`, in two views; re-run it after editing to update them in
place (the runs shown, filters and colours set in the UI are kept):
- **Compare setups:** grouped by `--exp-name`, one metric per chart, the mean over seeds with a min-max band. Sections,
  most telling first: verdict (validation nominal survival, pace, crashed completion, disturbed survival),
  generalization (train vs validation), robustness by tier, how it fails, driving, PPO health; then collapsed: PPO
  losses, clean by tier, the train split's tiers, perf. No reward charts: return changes meaning with the reward.
- **One run:** no grouping, related metrics overlaid (survival with its 90% interval over tracks, survival + completion
  + clean per tier, how episodes end, losses of control by cause, reward components, both action stds); tick one run.

**Comparing runs:** two evaluations share scenario ids. `compare.py A.csv B.csv [--tracks]` counts the scenarios A passes
and B fails, and the reverse, per tier, with a sign test. That compares two policies; to compare two setups, run 3 seeds
of each (`sweep.py`) and read `sweep_report.py`, which measures differences against the noise between seeds.

## How to

```sh
uv sync --all-extras                       # once
uv run pytest                              # ~20 s, needs the native build installed
uv run python scripts/train.py --exp-name first --total-timesteps 50000000
uv run python scripts/train.py --exp-name test --total-timesteps 3000000 --anneal-timesteps 10000000  # a 10M run's first 3M
uv run python scripts/wandb_workspace.py   # the W&B views' charts (after editing their layout)
uv run python scripts/eval.py runs/<run>/checkpoints/best.pt [--splits validation train] [--grid-laps]
uv run python scripts/compare.py runs/<A>/eval/<step>.csv runs/<B>/eval/<step>.csv --tracks
caffeinate -i uv run python scripts/sweep.py   # the reward sweep, resumable; --dry-run lists it
caffeinate -i uv run python scripts/sweep.py --only base --seeds 1 --steps 10000000   # one 10M run (eval every 2M)
uv run python scripts/sweep_report.py [--steps 10000000] [--curve]   # per variant over seeds, z against base
```

Each run writes these to `runs/<run>/`:
- `args.json`,
- `wandb/` (W&B's local copy of the metrics),
- `checkpoints/<step>.pt` and `best.pt` (the weights, the observation normalization, and the args),
- `eval/<step>.csv` (one row per scenario).

The native layer is rebuilt with `cmake --build native/build -j && cmake --install native/build` after any change to
`native/`.
